# MODULE: DISORDERS DUE TO SUBSTANCE USE

## 1. VARIABLE DICTIONARY

**Core Symptoms:**
- Sedation / drowsiness
- Slow breathing / respiratory failure
- Unresponsive / minimally responsive
- Pinpoint pupils
- Normal pupils
- Dilated pupils
- Tremors / shaking
- Sweating
- Nausea / vomiting / diarrhea
- Increased pulse / heart rate (tachycardia)
- Increased blood pressure
- Agitation / hyper-excitable state / restlessness
- Confusion / disorientation
- Hallucinations / racing thoughts / disordered thinking
- Aggressive / erratic / violent behavior
- Muscle aches / abdominal cramps
- Runny eyes and nose (rhinorrhea/lacrimation)
- Nystagmus (involuntary eye movement)
- Ophthalmoplegia (weakness/paralysis of eye muscles)
- Ataxia (uncoordinated movements)

**Secondary Symptoms / Clinical Signs:**
- Smell of alcohol on breath
- Slurred speech
- Uninhibited behavior
- Recent injection marks / skin infections
- Jaundiced (yellow) skin and eyes
- Palpable/tender liver edge / ascites / spider naevi
- Poor dentition / malnutrition
- Laboratory signs: elevated liver enzymes, elevated ammonia, macrocytic anaemia, low platelet count, elevated MCV

**Duration Thresholds & Usage Patterns:**
- *Recently stopped/used:* < 1 week
- *Drinking heavily:* Last few days
- *Alcohol Dependence indicator:* > 6 standard drinks at a time AND daily use
- *Tobacco Dependence indicator:* Several times a day, starting in the morning
- *Cannabis Dependence indicator:* At least 1g of cannabis daily
- *Prescription Pills Dependence indicator:* Taking higher doses than prescribed

**Demographic/Special Flags:**
- Adolescent
- Woman of child-bearing age
- Pregnant
- Breastfeeding
- Elderly
- Homeless / lacking social support
- Hepatic impairment / Liver disease (precautions for medication)
- Respiratory disease (precautions for sedation)

## 2. RED FLAGS & REFERRAL TRIGGERS

- **IF** the person is unresponsive, minimally responsive, or in respiratory failure, **THEN** Check ABCs, provide respiratory support, give oxygen, consider IV rehydration, and observe or transport to hospital.
- **IF** suspected Alcohol/Benzodiazepine Withdrawal AND the person has other serious medical problems (hepatic encephalopathy, GI bleeding, head injury), seizures, or severe delirium, **THEN** give initial diazepam/haloperidol and TRANSFER IMMEDIATELY TO HOSPITAL.
- **IF** suspected Acute Stimulant Intoxication AND the person has chest pain, tachyarrhythmias, or other neurological signs, **THEN** TRANSFER TO HOSPITAL.
- **IF** suicidal thoughts are present at any point (e.g., post-intoxication or during assessment), **THEN** REFER TO Suicide module (» SUI).
- **IF** psychotic symptoms persist after initial sedation in stimulant intoxication, **THEN** REFER TO Psychosis module (» PSY).
- **IF** aggressive and/or agitated behavior requires further management, **THEN** REFER TO Psychosis module (» PSY).
- **IF** confusion is explained by underlying medical conditions (head trauma, hypoglycaemia, pneumonia, hepatic encephalopathy, CVA), **THEN** manage the physical condition and REFER TO HOSPITAL.
- **IF** Wernicke's Encephalopathy is suspected (nystagmus, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia), **THEN** treat with thiamine and TRANSFER TO HOSPITAL.
- **IF** the patient is an adolescent and other priority mental health conditions are identified, **THEN** REFER TO Child and Adolescent Mental and Behavioural Disorders module (» CMH).

## 3. CLINICAL DECISION LOGIC (IF/THEN PATHS)

**PHASE 1: EMERGENCY ASSESSMENT**
- **STEP 1: Assess for Sedation**
  - **IF** the person appears sedated, **THEN** check pupils.
    - **IF** pupils are pinpoint, **THEN** Suspect OPIOID OVERDOSE. Give Naloxone. 
      - **IF** responds within 2 minutes, **THEN** observe 1-2 hours and repeat as needed. 
      - **ELSE**, give a second dose and observe until recovered or transported to hospital.
    - **IF** pupils are normal, **THEN** Suspect SEDATIVE INTOXICATION (alcohol, sedatives). Rule out opioid overdose, monitor vitals, provide supportive care.
  - **ELSE** (Not sedated), proceed to Step 2.

- **STEP 2: Assess for Overstimulation, Anxiety, or Agitation**
  - **IF** person recently stopped drinking/sedatives AND shows tremors, sweating, vomiting, increased BP/HR, or agitation, **THEN** Suspect ALCOHOL or BENZODIAZEPINE WITHDRAWAL. Treat with Diazepam (and thiamine for alcohol).
  - **IF** person recently used stimulants AND shows dilated pupils, anxiety, agitation, racing thoughts, or raised BP/HR, **THEN** Suspect ACUTE STIMULANT INTOXICATION. Treat with Diazepam.
  - **IF** person recently stopped opioids AND shows dilated pupils, muscle aches, cramps, nausea, diarrhea, runny eyes/nose, or restlessness, **THEN** Suspect ACUTE OPIOID WITHDRAWAL. Treat with Methadone or Buprenorphine.
  - **ELSE** (Not overstimulated), proceed to Step 3.

- **STEP 3: Assess for Confusion**
  - **IF** underlying medical conditions explain confusion, **THEN** transfer to hospital.
  - **IF** stopped drinking in the last week AND shows confusion, hallucinations, agitation, or disorientation, **THEN** Suspect ALCOHOL/BENZODIAZEPINE WITHDRAWAL DELIRIUM. Treat with Diazepam/Haloperidol and transfer to hospital.
  - **IF** drinking heavily in the last few days AND shows nystagmus, ophthalmoplegia, or ataxia, **THEN** Suspect WERNICKE’S ENCEPHALOPATHY. Treat with thiamine and transfer to hospital.
  - **IF** used stimulants in the last few days AND shows dilated pupils, strange/aggressive behavior, or disordered thinking, **THEN** Suspect STIMULANT INTOXICATION. Treat with Diazepam/Haloperidol.
  - **ELSE** (No emergency presentation), proceed to Phase 2 (Standard Assessment).

**PHASE 2: STANDARD ASSESSMENT (SUB 1)**
- **STEP 4: General Substance Use**
  - **IF** the person DOES NOT use psychoactive substances, **THEN** emphasize health benefits of non-use and EXIT MODULE.
  - **IF** the person DOES use substances, **THEN** proceed to Step 5.

- **STEP 5: Assess Harmful Use**
  - Ask if substance use causes harmful behaviors (injuries, relationship problems, legal/financial issues, poor role performance, risky sexual activity, injecting).
  - **IF** YES, **THEN** flag as HARMFUL USE and proceed to Step 6.
  - **IF** NO, **THEN** proceed to Step 6.

- **STEP 6: Assess Dependence**
  - Ask about high frequency/quantity, strong cravings, difficulty self-regulating, increased tolerance, and withdrawal symptoms.
  - **IF** YES (Dependence features present), **THEN** Diagnose DEPENDENCE -> Go to PROTOCOL 2.
    - *Sub-path Opioids:* Provide Opioid Agonist Maintenance (Protocol 5) or Opioid Withdrawal (Protocol 4).
    - *Sub-path Benzodiazepines:* Provide gradual reduction/Benzodiazepine Withdrawal (Protocol 6).
    - *Sub-path Alcohol:* Arrange Alcohol Withdrawal (Protocol 3) and relapse prevention.
    - *Sub-path Other Substances:* Advise complete cessation, provide psychosocial interventions.
  - **IF** NO (Dependence features absent) AND Harmful Use flag is YES, **THEN** Diagnose HARMFUL USE -> Go to PROTOCOL 1.
    - Provide psychoeducation, motivational interviewing, advice on stopping/reducing, address social needs, follow up in one month.
  - **IF** NO (Dependence features absent) AND Harmful Use flag is NO, **THEN** provide psychoeducation on risks of substance use and EXIT MODULE.

**PHASE 3: FOLLOW-UP (SUB 3)**
- **STEP 7: Review**
  - **IF** Harmful Use, **THEN** follow up in 1 month.
  - **IF** Dependence, **THEN** follow up several times per week initially, then weekly, then monthly.
  - **IF** ongoing use, **THEN** develop harm reduction strategies and arrange detoxification if client agrees.
  - **IF** recently ceased use, **THEN** give positive feedback, consider relapse prevention medications, and support social factors.

## 4. POSSIBLE OUTCOMES

**Diagnoses:**
- Sedative Intoxication (Alcohol, Opioids, Benzodiazepines)
- Opioid Overdose
- Acute Opioid Withdrawal
- Alcohol / Benzodiazepine Withdrawal
- Alcohol / Benzodiazepine Withdrawal Delirium
- Acute Stimulant Intoxication
- Wernicke’s Encephalopathy
- Harmful Use (Substance-specific)
- Dependence (Substance-specific)
- Neonatal Abstinence Syndrome (in infants of substance-using mothers)

**Rule-outs / No Diagnosis:**
- No Substance Use
- Non-Harmful Substance Use (Requires brief psychoeducation only)
- Physical/Medical cause of confusion (Requires rule-out of head trauma, infection, hypoglycemia, CVA, hepatic encephalopathy)
