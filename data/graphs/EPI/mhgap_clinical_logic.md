# MODULE: EPILEPSY (Convulsive Seizures)

## 1. VARIABLE DICTIONARY

- **Core Symptoms:**
  - Convulsive movements (stiffness, rigidity, shaking, non-purposeful movements)
  - Loss of consciousness or impaired consciousness

- **Secondary Symptoms:**
  - Bitten or bruised tongue
  - Bodily injury from fall/movement
  - Incontinence of faeces/urine
  - Post-ictal state (fatigue, drowsiness, sleepiness, confusion, abnormal behavior, headache, muscle aches, unilateral weakness)
  - Syncope signs (flushing, sweating, pallor, vision darkening) - *Used for rule-out*
  - Pseudoseizure signs (stress trigger, eyes closed, pelvic thrusting, rapid return to baseline) - *Used for rule-out*
  - Acute infection signs (fever, headache, confusion, stiff neck/meningeal irritation)

- **Duration Thresholds:**
  - > 1-2 minutes (Duration to consider a clinically significant convulsion)
  - 5 minutes (Emergency threshold for carers to seek immediate medical help)
  - 10 minutes (Wait time after 1st dose of emergency medication)
  - > 15 minutes (Threshold for "prolonged" febrile seizure in children)
  - > 50% (Target seizure frequency reduction for treatment success)
  - 2 years (Minimum seizure-free period before considering medication discontinuation)

- **Demographic/Special Flags:**
  - Woman of childbearing age
  - Pregnant (Second half of pregnancy)
  - Post-partum (Up to 1 week)
  - Child (6 months to 6 years old)
  - Child/Adolescent
  - Person living with HIV

## 2. RED FLAGS & REFERRAL TRIGGERS

- **IF** signs of head injury, neuroinfection (fever + stiff neck), or focal neurological deficits, **THEN REFER TO** urgent hospital evaluation.
- **IF** female in the second half of pregnancy OR up to 1 week post-partum AND has no prior history of epilepsy + presents with convulsions, **THEN REFER TO** hospital urgently (Suspect Eclampsia; administer Magnesium Sulphate prior).
- **IF** convulsions do not stop after 2 doses of emergency medication (Status Epilepticus), **THEN REFER TO** health facility urgently for continuous ABC/oxygen monitoring and IV anti-epileptic loading.
- **IF** child 6 months to 6 years old with fever has a focal, prolonged (> 15 min), or repetitive convulsion, **THEN REFER TO** hospital for admission (Complex Febrile Seizure).
- **IF** neurological exam reveals underlying causes (birth asphyxia, brain infection, trauma, focal deficits), **THEN REFER TO** specialist for diagnostic evaluation.
- **IF** imminent risk of suicide is identified during assessment, **THEN REFER TO** Suicide (SUI) module before continuing.
- **IF** the patient's seizures do not improve (>50% reduction) despite adherence, dose maximization, and medication switching, **THEN REFER TO** specialist.

## 3. CLINICAL DECISION LOGIC (IF/THEN PATHS)

### STEP 1: Emergency Acute Assessment
- **IF** the person presents with active convulsions or is unresponsive/stiff, **THEN** assess for head/neck injury. 
  - **IF** injury present, **THEN** keep head/neck stable. 
  - **ELSE** ensure airway/breathing/circulation (ABCs), check vitals, time the convulsion, and place person on their side.

### STEP 2: Eclampsia Rule-Out
- **IF** patient is female AND (in second half of pregnancy OR up to 1 week post-partum) AND has NO past history of epilepsy, **THEN** suspect Eclampsia. 
  - Give Magnesium Sulphate 10g IM. 
  - **IF** diastolic BP > 110 mmHg, **THEN** give Hydralazine 5mg IV slowly. 
  - URGENT REFERRAL.
- **ELSE** proceed to standard emergency medication (Step 3).

### STEP 3: Acute Seizure Termination
- **IF** IV is established, **THEN** give IV normal saline, IV glucose, AND Diazepam (10 mg IV adult; 1mg/yr child) OR Lorazepam (4 mg IV adult; 0.1 mg/kg child).
- **ELSE** give Diazepam rectally (10 mg adult; 1mg/yr child) OR Midazolam buccally/intranasally (5-10 mg adult; 0.2 mg/kg child).
- Wait 10 minutes.
- **IF** convulsions stop within 10 minutes, **THEN** proceed to EPI Assessment (Step 6).
- **ELSE** give a 2nd dose of emergency medication.
  - **IF** convulsions stop, **THEN** proceed to EPI Assessment.
  - **ELSE** (Status Epilepticus suspected) **THEN** URGENT REFERRAL. Give oxygen, monitor airway, and administer IV Valproic Acid, Phenobarbital, OR Phenytoin.

### STEP 4: Treat Acute Underlying Causes (Post-Convulsion)
- **IF** acute causes are present (neuroinfection, substance use/withdrawal, trauma, hypoglycemia/hyponatremia, stroke, tumor), **THEN** evaluate and treat specific underlying cause.
  - **IF** convulsions still do not stop, **THEN** refer to specialist.
  - **ELSE** proceed to EPI Assessment.

### STEP 5: Initial Epilepsy Assessment (EPI 1)
- **IF** convulsive movements lasted less than 1-2 minutes, OR patient lacks at least 2 associated symptoms (LOC, stiffness, incontinence, bite/injury, post-ictal state), **THEN** Convulsive Seizures unlikely (Consider syncope or pseudoseizures). Consult specialist if recurrent, follow up in 3 months.
- **ELSE IF** convulsive movements > 1-2 minutes AND has >= 2 associated symptoms, **THEN** Suspect Convulsive Seizures. Proceed to Step 6.

### STEP 6: Differentiating Acute vs Chronic Causes
- **IF** patient is a child 6 months to 6 years AND has a fever:
  - **IF** convulsions are focal, prolonged (>15 min), or repetitive, **THEN** suspect Complex Febrile Seizure -> REFER to hospital.
  - **ELSE** suspect Simple Febrile Seizure -> Look for infection cause, observe 24 hours, NO antiepileptic treatment needed.
- **IF** patient is older or has no fever but has other acute causes (meningitis, head injury, metabolic):
  - **THEN** evaluate/treat medical condition, REFER if necessary, NO antiepileptic medication required, follow up in 3 months.
- **ELSE IF** NO acute cause is present, **THEN** suspect Epilepsy. Proceed to Step 7.

### STEP 7: Confirming Epilepsy Diagnosis
- **IF** the person has had at least two unprovoked seizures on two different days in the past year, **THEN** Epilepsy is likely.
- **ELSE** patient does not meet criteria for epilepsy. Maintenance medication not necessary. Follow up in 3 months.

### STEP 8: Management and Special Populations (EPI 2)
- **IF** patient is a Woman of Childbearing Age, **THEN** advise Folate (5 mg/day). AVOID Valproate. If pregnant, avoid polytherapy and give Vitamin K 1mg IM to newborn at delivery. If breastfeeding, Carbamazepine is preferred.
- **IF** patient is a Child/Adolescent with a behavioral disorder, **THEN** AVOID Phenobarbital.
- **IF** patient is living with HIV, **THEN** Valproate is preferred. AVOID Phenytoin and Carbamazepine.
- **THEN** start ONE antiepileptic medication (Carbamazepine, Phenobarbital, Phenytoin, or Sodium Valproate) at the lowest starting dose and titrate slowly. Provide psychoeducation.

### STEP 9: Follow-Up & Titration (EPI 3)
- **IF** patient has > 50% seizure reduction, **THEN** continue current dose and monitor every 3-6 months.
- **ELSE IF** patient has < 50% seizure reduction, **THEN** review adherence -> increase dose to max -> switch medication if needed -> refer to specialist if still poor.
- **IF** patient has been convulsion-free for at least 2 years, **THEN** discuss risks/benefits of medication discontinuation. IF in agreement, taper doses slowly over 2 months. 

## 4. POSSIBLE OUTCOMES

- **Diagnoses:**
  - Epilepsy (Convulsive type)
  - Status Epilepticus
  - Eclampsia (Pregnancy/Post-partum complication)
  - Simple Febrile Seizure
  - Complex Febrile Seizure
- **Rule-outs / No Diagnosis:**
  - Convulsive Seizures Unlikely (e.g., Syncope, Pseudoseizures/Psychogenic non-epileptic seizures)
  - Does Not Meet Epilepsy Criteria (e.g., single isolated seizure, infrequent seizures < 2 per year)
  - Acute Symptomatic Seizures (due to head injury, stroke, metabolic imbalance, intoxication/withdrawal, neuroinfection without ongoing epilepsy)
