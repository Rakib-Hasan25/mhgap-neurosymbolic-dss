# MODULE: Child & Adolescent Mental & Behavioural Disorders (CMH)

## 1. VARIABLE DICTIONARY
**Core Symptoms:**
- **Developmental:** Delay in meeting milestones (smiling, sitting, walking, talking, toilet training), poor feeding, failure to thrive, delay in reading/writing/self-care, poor school performance, difficulty understanding instructions or adjusting to changes, restrictive/repetitive behaviors.
- **Inattention/Hyperactivity:** Overactive, excessive running/talking, unable to stay still, moving restlessly, easily distracted, absent-minded, repeatedly stopping tasks before completion, impulsive (acting without forethought).
- **Behavioral/Conduct:** Repeatedly aggressive, disobedient, or defiant; arguing with adults, extreme irritability/anger, frequent/severe tantrums, cruel behavior (to people/animals), excessive fighting/bullying, provocative behavior, destructiveness, fire-setting, stealing, repeated lying, truancy, running away.
- **Emotional:** Irritable/down/sad mood, lost interest or enjoyment, many worries/fears, excessive crying/clinging/freezing, unexplained physical symptoms (stomach-ache, headache, nausea), tearfulness, extreme shyness, reluctance/refusal to go to school, avoidance of specific situations/objects.

**Secondary Symptoms (Depression-specific in Adolescents):**
- Disturbed sleep (too much or too little)
- Significant change in appetite or weight
- Beliefs of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Fatigue or loss of energy
- Reduced concentration or indecisiveness
- Observable agitation or physical restlessness
- Talking or moving more slowly than usual
- Hopelessness
- Suicidal thoughts or acts

**Duration Thresholds:**
- **>= 6 months:** Required duration for ADHD and Conduct Disorder diagnoses.
- **Most days for the last 2 weeks:** Required duration for Depression symptoms in adolescents.
- **< 5 mins to 25 mins:** Developmentally typical duration for toddler tantrums (occurring < 3 times per week).

**Demographic/Special Flags:**
- Infants and Young Children (Age < 5)
- Middle Childhood (Age 6–12)
- Adolescents (Age 13–18)
- Carer with MNS conditions (e.g., depression, substance use)
- Suspected child maltreatment / neglect / abuse
- Concurrent physical illness (e.g., HIV/AIDS, malnutrition, anaemia, thyroid disease)

## 2. RED FLAGS & REFERRAL TRIGGERS
- **IF** Visual and/or hearing impairment is suspected, **THEN REFER TO** Specialist for evaluation.
- **IF** Imminent risk of suicide or self-harm is present, **THEN REFER TO** Suicide module (SUI).
- **IF** Signs of child maltreatment, abuse, or neglect are present, **THEN REFER TO** Child protection services, ensure immediate safety, and arrange specialist support.
- **IF** Concurrent priority MNS conditions exist (e.g., Substance use, Epilepsy), **THEN REFER TO** Substance Use (SUB) or Epilepsy (EPI) modules.
- **IF** A history of manic episode(s) is identified, **THEN REFER TO** Depression module (DEP) rules.
- **IF** Physical conditions mimicking ADHD (thyroid, HIV, uncontrolled pain) or Emotional Disorders (thyroid, anaemia, malnutrition, asthma, medication side-effects) are suspected, **THEN** Treat/manage the physical condition first/concurrently.
- **IF** ADHD diagnosis is confirmed, patient is >= 6 years old, and psychosocial treatments have failed, **THEN REFER TO** Specialist for methylphenidate treatment.
- **IF** Moderate/Severe Depression is confirmed, patient is >= 12 years old, and psychological treatments have failed after 6 months, **THEN REFER TO** Specialist for Fluoxetine (DO NOT prescribe other SSRIs or TCAs).
- **IF** No improvement in symptoms/functioning across any disorder after 6 months of psychosocial interventions, **THEN REFER TO** Specialist.

## 3. CLINICAL DECISION LOGIC (IF/THEN PATHS)

- **STEP 1: ASSESS FOR DEVELOPMENTAL DISORDERS**
  - **IF** the child has difficulties with age-appropriate milestones (motor, cognitive, social, communication, adaptive) OR older child/adolescent has difficulties with school, communication, self-care, **THEN** Suspect DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY/DISORDER.
    - **IF** signs suggest nutritional deficiency, anaemia, malnutrition, or infectious illness (including HIV/AIDS), **THEN** Manage conditions using IMCI guidelines.
    - **IF** child fails vision or hearing assessments, **THEN** Consult Specialist.
    - **ELSE** Go to PROTOCOL 1 (Developmental Delay/Disorder).
  - **ELSE** (No developmental issues), SKIP to STEP 2.

- **STEP 2: ASSESS FOR PROBLEMS WITH INATTENTION OR HYPERACTIVITY**
  - **IF** the child/adolescent is overactive, unable to stay still, easily distracted, or moving restlessly, **THEN** assess severity.
    - **IF** symptoms are present in multiple settings AND lasted >= 6 months AND are inappropriate for developmental level AND cause considerable daily difficulty, **THEN** Consider ADHD.
      - **IF** physical conditions resembling ADHD are present (thyroid, HIV, uncontrolled pain), **THEN** Treat the physical condition, then Go to PROTOCOL 3.
      - **ELSE** Go to PROTOCOL 3 (ADHD).
    - **ELSE IF** symptoms do not meet all above criteria, **THEN** Consider PROBLEMS WITH BEHAVIOUR -> Go to PROTOCOL 2.
  - **ELSE** (No inattention/hyperactivity), SKIP to STEP 3.

- **STEP 3: ASSESS FOR CONDUCT DISORDER**
  - **IF** the child/adolescent shows repeated aggressive, disobedient, or defiant behavior, **THEN** assess severity.
    - **IF** symptoms are present in multiple settings AND lasted >= 6 months AND are more severe than ordinary mischief/rebelliousness AND cause considerable daily difficulty, **THEN** Consider CONDUCT DISORDER -> Go to PROTOCOL 4.
    - **ELSE IF** symptoms do not meet all criteria, **THEN** Consider PROBLEMS WITH BEHAVIOUR -> Go to PROTOCOL 2.
  - **ELSE** (No repeated aggressive/defiant behavior), Conduct Disorder is unlikely -> SKIP to STEP 4.

- **STEP 4: ASSESS FOR EMOTIONAL DISORDERS**
  - **IF** the child/adolescent often feels irritable/sad, has lost interest, has many fears/worries, or complains of unexplained physical sickness, **THEN** assess impact.
    - **IF** there is considerable difficulty with daily functioning, **THEN** Consider EMOTIONAL DISORDER.
      - **IF** signs suggest a physical condition exacerbating emotions (thyroid, HIV, obesity, anaemia, asthma, medication side-effects), **THEN** Manage the physical condition.
      - **IF** the patient is an Adolescent (13-18), **THEN** assess for moderate to severe depression.
        - **IF** adolescent has irritable/down mood OR lost interest AND has several secondary depression symptoms (sleep, appetite, guilt, fatigue, concentration, agitation, suicidal thoughts) most days for the last 2 weeks, **THEN** Consider DEPRESSION -> Rule out bipolar/mania -> Go to PROTOCOL 6.
        - **ELSE** Go to PROTOCOL 6 (Emotional Disorder).
      - **ELSE** (Not an adolescent), Go to PROTOCOL 6.
    - **ELSE IF** there is no considerable difficulty with daily functioning, **THEN** Consider PROBLEMS WITH EMOTIONS -> Go to PROTOCOL 5.
  - **ELSE** (No emotional distress), SKIP to STEP 5.

- **STEP 5: ASSESS FOR OTHER PRIORITY MNS CONDITIONS & ENVIRONMENT**
  - **IF** concurrent MNS conditions exist (suicide risk, substance use, epilepsy), **THEN** Assess and manage concurrently.
  - **IF** problems are a reaction to a distressing situation/maltreatment, **THEN** Refer to child protection, explore stressors, ensure safety.
  - **IF** carers have a priority MNS condition (e.g., depression), **THEN** Assess and manage the carer.
  - **IF** child/adolescent is out of school, bullied, or refusing school, **THEN** Liaise with teachers and explore reasons for absence.

- **STEP 6: FOLLOW-UP AND MONITORING**
  - **IF** the patient is improving, **THEN** continue management plan; consider tapering meds if symptom-free for 9-12 months.
  - **IF** the patient is NOT improving after 6 months of psychosocial interventions, **THEN**:
    - For Developmental / Conduct Disorders: Refer to Specialist (DO NOT use pharmacology).
    - For ADHD (>= 6 years old): Refer to Specialist for methylphenidate.
    - For Emotional Disorders (< 12 years old): Refer to Specialist (DO NOT initiate pharmacology).
    - For Depression (>= 12 years old): Consult Specialist for fluoxetine.

## 4. POSSIBLE OUTCOMES
**Diagnoses:**
- Developmental Delay/Disorder
- Problems with Behaviour
- Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)
- Conduct Disorder
- Problems with Emotions
- Emotional Disorder
- Moderate to Severe Depression (Adolescents only)

**Rule-outs / No Diagnosis:**
- Age-Appropriate Behaviors (e.g., typical toddler tantrums, adolescent limit-testing, age-typical fears)
- Underlying Physical Illness / Medication Side Effects
- Vision or Hearing Impairment
- Bipolar Disorder (Rule out manic episodes before diagnosing Depression)
- Environmental Stress Reaction (Behavior strictly due to maltreatment/abuse/acute stress)
