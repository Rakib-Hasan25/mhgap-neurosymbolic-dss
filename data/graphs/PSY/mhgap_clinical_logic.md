# MODULE: PSYCHOSES (Psychosis and Bipolar Disorder)

## 1. VARIABLE DICTIONARY

- **Core Symptoms:**
  - *Psychosis specific:* Hallucinations (hearing voices, seeing things), Delusions (fixed false beliefs not shared by culture), Disorganized speech and/or behaviour (incoherent/irrelevant speech, mumbling, laughing to self, strange appearance, self-neglect, unkempt).
  - *Bipolar (Manic) specific:* Elevated or irritable mood, Decreased need for sleep, Increased activity / feeling of increased energy, Increased talkativeness / rapid speech, Loss of normal social inhibitions (e.g., sexual indiscretion), Impulsive or reckless behaviours (e.g., excessive spending, making important decisions without planning), Distractibility, Unrealistically inflated self-esteem.
- **Secondary Symptoms:** 
  - Marked behavioural changes, neglecting usual responsibilities.
  - Agitation, aggressive behaviour, inactivity, or hyperactivity.
  - Disturbances of emotion (marked apathy, disconnect between reported emotion and facial/body expression).
  - Lack of realization that one is having mental health problems (lack of insight).
- **Duration Thresholds:**
  - **>= 1 week:** Minimum duration for manic episode symptoms to be considered diagnostic.
  - **2-4 weeks maximum:** Duration limit for short-term benzodiazepine use for agitation.
  - **4-6 weeks:** Timeframe required on an effective typical dose to determine medication efficacy.
  - **12 months:** Period of symptom resolution required before considering discontinuation of antipsychotics for a first episode/relapse of psychosis.
  - **>= 2 years:** Minimum duration of full remission (no episodes) before considering discontinuation of maintenance medications for bipolar disorder.
  - **Several years:** Period of full remission required before considering discontinuation of medications for a person with persistent psychotic symptoms (> 3 months).
- **Demographic/Special Flags:**
  - **Pregnant or Breastfeeding:** AVOID Valproate, Lithium, Carbamazepine, anticholinergics, and depot antipsychotics. Consider low-dose oral haloperidol or chlorpromazine.
  - **Women of childbearing age / Planning pregnancy:** Weigh risks/benefits; avoid depot antipsychotics.
  - **Adolescents:** Risperidone is the preferred option (under specialist supervision); alternatives are haloperidol or chlorpromazine.
  - **Older Adults:** Use lower doses; anticipate drug-drug interactions; CAUTION regarding increased risk of cerebrovascular events/death with antipsychotics in dementia-related psychosis.
  - **Persons living with HIV/AIDS:** Valproate is the preferred mood stabilizer due to fewer antiretroviral drug-drug interactions.

## 2. RED FLAGS & REFERRAL TRIGGERS

- **IF** there is an imminent risk of suicide, **THEN REFER TO** Suicide (SUI) module.
- **IF** the person presents with extreme violence, **THEN** seek immediate help from police/staff, administer IM haloperidol, consult a specialist, and **REFER TO** hospital if agitation persists.
- **IF** there are signs/symptoms of an acute physical condition causing delirium (e.g., infection, cerebral malaria, dehydration, hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia) or medication side effects, **THEN** manage the acute physical condition and **REFER TO** emergency services or a specialist as needed.
- **IF** the agitation is due to ingestion of substances (alcohol/sedative withdrawal or stimulant intoxication), **THEN REFER TO** Substance Use (SUB) module.
- **IF** the patient presents with a Depressive Episode of Bipolar Disorder, **THEN REFER TO** Depression (DEP) module.
- **IF** the patient requires Lithium but clinical/laboratory monitoring is unavailable, **THEN REFER TO** a specialist or utilize alternative medications (Valproate, Carbamazepine).
- **IF** the patient experiences severe Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS) or prolonged QT interval/arrhythmias from antipsychotics, **THEN** discontinue and **REFER TO** emergency medical care.

## 3. CLINICAL DECISION LOGIC (IF/THEN PATHS)

- **STEP 1: INITIAL AGITATION & MEDICAL RULE-OUT**
  - **IF** the person presents with acute agitation and/or aggression, **THEN** check vital signs and blood glucose.
    - **IF** blood glucose is low, **THEN** administer glucose.
    - **IF** hypoxia is present, **THEN** give oxygen.
  - **IF** delirium, poisoning, or an acute physical condition is suspected, **THEN** treat the underlying cause and assess whether psychiatric symptoms persist.
  - **IF** drug/alcohol intoxication or withdrawal is suspected, **THEN** treat with Diazepam (if applicable) and branch to SUB module.
  - **IF** physical and substance causes are ruled out and symptoms persist, **THEN** proceed to STEP 2.

- **STEP 2: DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS (CONCURRENT CONDITIONS)**
  - **IF** symptoms suggest Dementia or Depression, **THEN** consult a specialist, manage concurrent conditions (branch to respective modules), and proceed to STEP 3.
  - **ELSE**, proceed directly to STEP 3.

- **STEP 3: ASSESS FOR ACUTE MANIC EPISODE (BIPOLAR DISORDER)**
  - **IF** several manic symptoms (elevated/irritable mood, decreased sleep, increased energy/speech, impulsivity, inflated self-esteem, etc.) occur simultaneously AND have lasted for **>= 1 week** AND are severe enough to interfere with work/social activities or require hospitalization:
    - **IF** there is imminent suicide risk, **THEN** exit to SUI module.
    - **ELSE**, Diagnose **BIPOLAR DISORDER (Manic Episode)** and initiate Management Protocol 1 (Step 5).
  - **ELSE**, proceed to STEP 4.

- **STEP 4: ASSESS FOR PSYCHOSIS**
  - **IF** the person has at least TWO of the following: (1) Delusions, (2) Hallucinations, (3) Disorganized speech and/or behaviour:
    - **IF** there is imminent suicide risk, **THEN** exit to SUI module.
    - **ELSE**, Diagnose **PSYCHOSIS** and initiate Management Protocol 2 (Step 6).
  - **ELSE**, consult with a specialist to review other possible causes of psychosis (Rule-Out).

- **STEP 5: MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL 1 (BIPOLAR MANIA)**
  - **IF** the patient is currently taking antidepressants, **THEN** DISCONTINUE them immediately to prevent worsening mania.
  - **IF** the patient is pregnant or breastfeeding, **THEN** AVOID Valproate, Lithium, and Carbamazepine. Consider low-dose haloperidol.
  - **ELSE**, begin treatment with a mood stabilizer (Lithium, Valproate, Carbamazepine) OR an antipsychotic (Haloperidol, Risperidone).
  - **IF** the patient experiences severe behavioural disturbances/agitation, **THEN** add a short-term benzodiazepine (maximum 2-4 weeks) and discontinue gradually.
  - Proceed to Step 7 (Follow-up).

- **STEP 6: MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL 2 (PSYCHOSIS)**
  - **IF** the patient is pregnant/breastfeeding, **THEN** avoid depot antipsychotics and anticholinergics. Consider low-dose oral haloperidol or chlorpromazine.
  - **ELSE**, start one antipsychotic medication immediately at the lowest dose and titrate up slowly. 
  - Proceed to Step 7 (Follow-up).

- **STEP 7: FOLLOW-UP & TREATMENT RESPONSE EVALUATION**
  - **IF** the person is improving:
    - **THEN** continue the treatment plan, decrease follow-up frequency, and proceed to Step 8 (Discontinuation assessment).
  - **IF** the person is NOT improving AND is NOT taking medication:
    - **THEN** start/restart medication, discuss adherence, and maintain high frequency of contact.
  - **IF** the person is NOT improving AND HAS been taking medication at a typical effective dose for **4-6 weeks**:
    - **THEN** verify dosing, check for substance use (branch to SUB), check for extrapyramidal/metabolic side effects, and consider switching to a different medication. 
    - **IF** still unresponsive after switching, **THEN** consider combination treatment or Clozapine (under strict specialist supervision) and consult a specialist.

- **STEP 8: MEDICATION DISCONTINUATION LOGIC**
  - **IF** Diagnosis is PSYCHOSIS (First episode or relapse) AND symptoms have been fully resolved for **12 months**, **THEN** consider gradual discontinuation of medications.
  - **IF** Diagnosis is PSYCHOSIS (Symptoms persisted > 3 months initially) AND the person has been in full remission for **several years**, **THEN** consider gradual discontinuation of medications.
  - **IF** Diagnosis is BIPOLAR DISORDER AND the person has been in full remission with no episodes for at least **2 years**, **THEN** consult a specialist and consider gradual discontinuation.
  - **ELSE**, routinely follow up and continue maintenance medications.

## 4. POSSIBLE OUTCOMES

- **Diagnoses:**
  - Bipolar Disorder (Acute Manic Episode)
  - Psychosis
- **Rule-outs / No Diagnosis:**
  - Delirium secondary to an acute physical condition (e.g., malaria, metabolic abnormality)
  - Medication-induced psychotic/manic symptoms (e.g., steroids, antimalarials)
  - Dementia (Referred)
  - Depression (Referred - DEP module)
  - Substance/Alcohol Intoxication or Withdrawal (Referred - SUB module)
  - No MNS condition diagnosed (Did not meet threshold for Bipolar Mania or Psychosis; requires specialist differential)
