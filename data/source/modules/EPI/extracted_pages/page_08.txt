**CLINICAL TIP**

Syncope and pseudoseizures should be considered during initial evaluation and in cases of treatment failure.

» Syncopal (fainting) spells often are associated with flushing, sweating, pallor, and occasionally a feeling of vision darkening prior to an episode. Mild shaking may occur at the end.

» Pseudoseizures are typically associated with a stress trigger. Episodes are often prolonged and can involve nonrhythmic jerking of the body, eyes may be closed, and pelvic thrusting is often seen. There is typically a rapid return to baseline after the episode. If pseudoseizures are suspected, go to » **OTH**.

**COMMON PRESENTATIONS OF EPILEPSY**

*   Convulsive movement or fits/seizures
    During the convulsion:
    – Loss of consciousness or impaired consciousness
    – Stiffness, rigidity
    – Tongue bite, injury, incontinence of urine or faeces
*   After the convulsion: fatigue, drowsiness, sleepiness, confusion, abnormal behaviour, headache, muscle aches, or weakness on one side of the body
